# Stay

> Their hour deserves your hour. Stay in it.

This document is the source of truth for what Stay is, who it's for, and how we talk about it. If a decision about the product, the copy, or the design needs a tiebreaker, the answer is in here. If the answer *isn't* in here and the decision matters, this document should grow.

---

## What Stay does

Stay locks your screen to your active meeting window. While Stay is on, switching to another app requires a small friction step (e.g. a password). The goal is to make focused presence the easy default instead of an act of constant willpower.

## What Stay is really about

Stay is about **reciprocity of attention.**

The other people on the call gave you an hour of their finite life. The honest response is to give them yours back, whole. Stay is a small tool that helps you keep that gesture.

It is not a productivity tool. It is a tool for honoring a small mutual commitment between adults.

---

## Core principles

These are the load-bearing commitments. Every product, design, and copy decision should be checkable against them.

### 1. Opt-in, always

Stay is something the user hands their keys to — never something done to them. No manager dashboards. No team-wide enforcement. No productivity scores. No compliance features. No surveillance. The moment Stay becomes a tool deployed *on* someone instead of *by* someone, it stops being Stay.

### 2. The user is a capable adult

Stay assumes the user already knows they multitask through meetings and doesn't like that about themselves. They don't need to be lectured, motivated, or saved. They need a small structure they asked for. Copy and UI treat them accordingly.

### 3. Quiet by default

The product runs in the background. The brand speaks softly. The marketing doesn't shout. If a feature or a line of copy is loud, it's probably wrong.

### 4. The gesture is the point

The mechanical lock is incidental. The meaningful thing is what the user is signaling — to themselves and to the people on the call — by choosing to use Stay. Every surface of the product should reinforce that what's happening is a small, voluntary act of respect.

### 5. Less, not more

Stay is about narrowing, not expanding. The product should feel like *relief*, not *restriction*. When in doubt, remove.

---

## Audience

Thoughtful working adults — knowledge workers, founders, designers, ICs, managers — who already know they multitask through meetings and want to be the kind of colleague they'd want to have.

Not for: people who need to be policed, teams whose managers want to deploy attention-monitoring tools, productivity-hack culture.

## Voice and tone

### Voice

- Warm, but not soft
- Mindful, but not preachy
- Plain-spoken — short sentences, real words
- Quietly confident — doesn't oversell, doesn't moralize

### The test

If a line sounds like it could appear on a LinkedIn post about "deep work" or in a meditation-app push notification, cut it. If it sounds like a thoughtful friend saying something true, keep it.

### Words and phrases we avoid

- "Crush," "level up," "10x," "unlock your potential"
- "Mindfulness," "be present" (the concept is fine; the worn phrasing isn't)
- "Distraction-free" (negative framing — we're for something, not against something)
- "Productivity," "efficiency," "performance"
- Anything that implies the user is broken and we're the fix

### Words and phrases that feel right

- Stay, hour, room, here, with, whole, keep
- "Show up," "the people on the call," "the hour you agreed to"
- Concrete nouns over abstract ones

## Copy

### Primary

**Name:** Stay
**Tagline:** Their hour deserves your hour. Stay in it.

### Secondary lines

For app store copy, social posts, onboarding, email subject lines. Use the primary tagline as the anchor; these expand the world.

- Show up the way you meant to.
- One window. One conversation.
- Stay with people who chose to be here.
- Be in the meeting you're in.
- Keep the hour you agreed to.
- The meeting deserves the meeting.

### How to talk about the product

- "I'm on Stay." / "I'm using Stay."
- "Turn on Stay." / "Stay is on."
- "Stay through the call." (verb usage — fine and encouraged)
- Avoid: "Stay locks you out of...", "Stay forces you to...", "Stay blocks distractions" — all true, all the wrong framing.

## Visual direction

Working notes, not prescriptions. Pin down with a designer when the time comes; until then, these are the guardrails.

- **Palette:** soft, warm neutrals. One quiet accent color. Avoid the Calm gradient. Avoid productivity-app blue.
- **Type:** considered but not precious. A humanist sans, or a serif used confidently. Nothing that screams "tech startup."
- **Layout:** generous whitespace. The product is about less, so the design should feel like less.
- **Motif worth exploring:** a single closed door, a single lit window, a single chair. Something that evokes *this room, this hour.*
- **What to avoid:** lock icons (too punitive), brain icons (too productivity-y), shield icons (too defensive), gradient meshes (too meditation-app).

## Macro goals

The long-horizon things this brand is trying to do. If any future decision conflicts with one of these, the decision is probably wrong.

1. **Make focused presence the easy default**, not the hard choice. Stay wins when using it feels like relief.

2. **Build the brand as a marker of intent.** Long-term, "I'm on Stay" should signal something about the kind of meeting participant you are. The product is the artifact; the gesture is the brand.

3. **Stay opt-in, always.** See Core Principle 1. This is the line that doesn't move.

4. **Earn trust by being quiet.** The customer should feel respected by Stay before they feel anything else.

5. **Grow through gesture, not hype.** People should adopt Stay because they noticed a colleague using it and thought *I want to be that kind of colleague.* The product is its own best advertisement when it's working.
